# E2E restoration benchmark — decode backends

- Date: 2026-07-21
- Version: 0.8.2 (unreleased)
- **BASELINE: the `vali` column is the reference baseline for future e2e runs.**

## Setup

- Script: `scripts/benchmark_decode_backends.py` (full `jasna` CLI per run, fresh subprocess, `DECODE_BACKEND` patched)
- Settings: `--max-clip-size 180 --temporal-overlap 15 --secondary-restoration none`, fp16, batch 4, defaults otherwise (detection model rfdetr-v6)
- Clips: `assets/benchmark/` (gitignored) — `SONE-610_bench_*` re-encoded from one 4K60 hevc 10-bit master, 3 concatenated segments (3:22 total, 29.97fps; hevc/av1 via NVENC `-preset p7 -tune hq`, h264 via libx264). One variant per codec matching real-world content: hevc/av1 10-bit, h264 8-bit. `vr1_8k_vr_hevc_8bit_60fps.mp4` = 8K VR (8192x4096 hevc 8-bit 59.94fps, 15s), vali/pyav-hw only.
- Hardware: dev box, NVIDIA driver 595.84, CUDA 13.0, Blackwell NVDEC
- Repeats: 1 (sub-2% deltas ≈ noise)

## Conclusions

1. vali wins every row: ~0-1% over pyav-hw at 720p/1080p, 3-6% at 4K.
2. pyav-sw costs 5-13% e2e; worst on 10-bit clips.
3. E2e is detection+restoration-bound: decode-only gap (vali 681 vs sw 179 fps at 4K) compresses to single digits e2e.
4. Throughput ≈ 155 fps at 1080p, ≈ 76 fps at 4K; resolution dominates, codec/bit depth barely matter with hw decode.
