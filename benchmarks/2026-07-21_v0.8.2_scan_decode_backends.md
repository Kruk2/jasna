- Date: 2026-07-21
- Version: 0.8.2 (unreleased)
- **BASELINE: the `vali` column is the reference baseline for future scan runs.**

## Setup

- Script: `scripts/benchmark_scan_backends.py` — GUI `MosaicScanWorker` with `stride_seconds=0.0` (detection on every frame, results into one preallocated GPU tensor, one CPU sync at the end). Detector build + engine compile excluded from timing; wall covers decode + detection + final sync.
- Settings: GUI defaults (rfdetr-v6, fp16, batch 4)
- Clips: same set as the e2e benchmark (`assets/benchmark/`, see e2e doc)
- RAM/VRAM: med/peak of the benchmark subprocess (0.5s sampling, `scripts/bench_memory.py`)

## Conclusions

1. Unlike full e2e, the scan is decode-sensitive: vali beats pyav-hw by 5-8% at ≤1080p, 13-31% at 4K (biggest: av1 4K +31%), 22% at 8K VR.
2. pyav-sw is 2-5x slower — software decode fully bottlenecks a detection-only pass.
3. vali also uses ~100-350 MB less VRAM than pyav-hw on every clip.
4. Scan throughput (vali): ~830-860 fps at ≤1080p, ~490-720 fps at 4K, ~170 fps at 8K VR.

## Parallel NVDEC decode (added same day)

RTX 5090 has 2 NVDEC units; every-frame scans of 4K+ material were decode-bound.
The scan now splits such videos across 2 decoder threads
(`scan_decoder_count`, every-frame + ≥4K + ≥10s + NVIDIA only). Vali results
after (exact result parity verified vs single decoder):

| Clip | Before fps | After fps | VRAM med/peak MB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720p h264 8bit | 860.6 | 863.3 | 1094/1094 |
| 1080p h264 8bit | 828.3 | 845.9 | 1214/1214 |
| 1080p hevc 10bit | 833.6 | 839.2 | 1276/1276 |
| 2160p h264 8bit | 716.5 | 727.8 | 2182/2182 |
| 2160p hevc 10bit | 532.6 | **711.2** | 2792/2792 |
| 2160p av1 10bit | 486.8 | **707.3** | 2718/2718 |
| 8K VR hevc 8bit 60fps | 168.7 | **217.5** | 5256/5256 |

All 4K now sits at the detection-inference ceiling (~730-770 fps at batch 4;
batch 8/16 engines measured slower: 799/691 fps vs 899 at batch 4). Further
scan gains require a faster detection engine, not decode work. Raw data:
`2026-07-21_v0.8.2_scan_parallel_decode.csv`, `..._scan_parallel_decode_vr.csv`.

Strided (interval) scans also use the parallel decoders (4097c01) — NVDEC
decodes every frame regardless of stride, so they were decode-bound as well.
1s-interval scan of the 4K hevc 10-bit clip: 11.1s → 5.7s (543 → 1067
video-frames/s, +96%); 8K VR 15s clip: 5.1s → 3.9s (+32%). Stride sample
phase re-anchors at the segment boundary (sample count/spacing preserved
within one stride).

Gating decision: the 2-decoder gate stays static (NVIDIA + ≥4K + ≥10s) with
no NVDEC-unit-count detection — no NVML/CUDA/Video Codec SDK API exposes the
engine count, and session oversubscription measured free-to-beneficial
(in-process decode sessions on this 2-unit GPU: 1→576, 2→1026, 3→1161,
4→1177 fps aggregate; the driver fair-shares sessions across engines). On a
1-NVDEC card two sessions are therefore neutral-to-slightly-positive, never a
regression; the only cost is ~200-400 MB VRAM for the extra session.
SCAN_PARALLEL_DECODERS=3 would buy strided scans another ~9% if ever wanted.
